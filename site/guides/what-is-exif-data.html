What is EXIF data, and what does it reveal about you?

EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) is a block of hidden information that cameras and phones write into every photo. You never see it when you look at the picture, but anyone with the original file can read it in seconds.

What a typical phone photo contains

FieldExampleWhat it gives away
GPS latitude and longitude21.2766, -157.8269Where the photo was taken, often to within a few metres. Taken at home, that is your address.
Date and time, time zone2026:07:04 14:32:10, -10:00When you were there, and roughly where in the world.
Make and modelGoogle Pixel 10 ProWhat phone you own. Combined with other photos, it can link pictures to the same person.
Lens and camera settingsf/1.68, 1/640 s, ISO 50Mostly harmless, but adds to a fingerprint of your device.
SoftwareHDR+ 1.0The app or firmware that processed the photo.
XMP and IPTC tagsEdits, captions, authorAdded by editing apps; can include names, places and edit history.
Data after the picturePreview images, Motion Photo clipExtra images or a short video stored after the photo itself, and a preview can carry its own location.

Videos carry similar information in their container, including a location and the time they were recorded.

Who can see it?

Anyone who receives the original file. Some social networks remove metadata when you upload, but many other routes keep it: email attachments, files sent as documents in messaging apps, cloud storage links, USB transfers and many websites that host the original image. You often can't tell which one you are using.
