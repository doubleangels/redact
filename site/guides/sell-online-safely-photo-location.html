Selling online? Remove the location from your photos first

The photos in a listing are usually taken at home: the sofa in the living room, the bike in the garage, the console on the TV stand. If they still carry GPS data, they can lead a stranger to your door, along with a list of what's worth taking.

Why this happens

Phones store the exact place each photo was taken inside the file as EXIF metadata. Some marketplaces remove it when you upload; others, and many of the ways buyers ask for "more photos" (email, messaging apps sending files, cloud links), pass the original file along with its location. You rarely know which is which.

A five-minute checklist

  1. Clean every photo before you post or send it. In Redact, select the photos on the Clean tab and tap Clean Metadata, or share them to Redact from your gallery. Post the clean copies.
  2. Check one to be sure. Open a clean copy in Scan: no Location badge, no GPS rows.
  3. Look at the picture itself. House numbers, street signs, the view from a window, letters with your address, and car number plates give away just as much.
  4. Keep conversations on the platform. Use the marketplace's messages until you've agreed a deal.
  5. Meet somewhere public for the hand-over when you can, rather than at home.
Sending extra photos to a buyer over chat or email? Share them to Redact first: it cleans them and reopens the share sheet with the clean copies, so you can send them straight on.

Does Redact upload my photos to clean them?

No. Everything happens on your phone, with no internet connection needed, no account and no ads.
