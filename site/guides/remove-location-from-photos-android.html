How to remove location from photos on Android

Most phones save the exact GPS spot where each photo was taken inside the file itself. Anyone who gets the original file can read it and put a pin on a map. Here is how to stop it, and how to clean the photos you already have.

Why it matters

The location is stored as EXIF metadata, hidden inside the image file. It is usually precise enough to find a building. Alongside it sit the date and time the photo was taken and your phone's make and model. (More on what EXIF data contains.)

Some apps and websites strip this data when you upload, but you can't count on it. Emailed photos, files sent as documents, cloud sharing links and files copied to other devices typically arrive with every hidden detail intact.

Step 1: Stop new photos saving your location

Open your camera app's settings and turn off the option that saves location. Depending on your phone it is called something like Location tags, Save location or Location. You can also take location access away from the camera app in Android's Settings › Apps › (your camera app) › Permissions.

This only affects new photos. Everything already in your gallery keeps its location, and some features (like browsing photos by place) stop working for new photos.

Step 2: Remove the location from photos you already have

Before you share an existing photo, send a clean copy instead. With Redact, which runs entirely on your phone:

  1. Check it. Open Redact, go to Scan and pick the photo. Rows marked Location show the GPS data inside it.
  2. Clean it. Go to Clean, select up to 20 photos or videos, and tap Clean Metadata. Redact saves clean copies and tells you what it removed, for example “Removed 24 metadata fields, including location.”
  3. Share the clean copy. Tap Share, or find the copies in Pictures/Redact and Movies/Redact. Your originals are not changed.
Fastest way: in your gallery or any other app, tap Share and pick Redact. It cleans the photo and reopens the share sheet with the clean copy, so you can send it straight to the person or app you meant to.

Step 3: Check that it worked

After cleaning one file, tap Scan Result in Redact to open the clean copy in Scan. There should be no Location badge and no GPS, Make or Model fields left.

What about videos?

Videos can carry a location too, stored in the video container rather than in EXIF. Redact cleans videos the same way: select them on the Clean tab, or share them to Redact.

Questions

Does removing the location lower the photo's quality?

Redact is designed to keep photos looking the same. Strict Clean and full video re-encodes re-encode the file, which can change some encoding details.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere to be cleaned?

No. Redact does everything on your phone and needs no internet connection.
